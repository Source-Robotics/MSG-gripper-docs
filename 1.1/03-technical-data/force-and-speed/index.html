    Force and speed¶

    A move command carries a current limit in mA and a speed from 0 to 255. This page says what they mean in newtons and millimetres per second.

    Grip force¶

    The driver limits the motor's current to the value you command, so the torque the motor produces is limited to \(T = K_t \, I_q\), with \(K_t\) the motor's torque constant in Nm/A (#Kt prints what the driver uses) and \(I_q\) the current limit in amperes (the command is in mA). The pinion turns the torque into the force the jaws press with:

    \[ F = \frac{K_t \, I_q}{r}, \qquad r = 8.3\ \text{mm} \]

    \(r\) is the pitch radius of the pinion (32 DP, 21 teeth). The force is proportional to the current, with a slope set by the stepper:

    Stepper Motor \(K_t\) Force per ampere Maximum current Grip force at the maximum current
    21.5 mm 17HS08-1004S 0.17 Nm/A 20 N/A 1.5 A about 31 N
    40 mm 17HS16-2004S1 0.21 Nm/A 25 N/A 2.0 A about 50 N
    60 mm 17HS24-2104S 0.30 Nm/A 36 N/A 2.1 A about 76 N

    The curves are computed from the torque constants, not measured with a force gauge: a measurement on your gripper is the only exact calibration, and a self-built gripper with another stepper has another \(K_t\). Below about 150 mA the motor does not move reliably, so the usable range starts at a few newtons. Motor GUI commands up to 1500 mA; the full force of the 40 mm and 60 mm steppers needs a command from Python or over CAN.

    The force is a limit, not a controlled value: a hard object takes the full force the moment the jaws touch it; a soft object is squeezed until the force is reached.

    Stay within the stepper's maximum

    The firmware does not clamp the gripper's current. A current above the stepper's maximum overheats the motor; the thermistor stops the gripper at 75 °C, but a motor driven far over its rating heats faster than the thermistor follows. The maximums are in the table above.

    Payload¶

    The grip force is not the weight the gripper can lift. An object held by friction between the two jaws is held by \(2\,\mu F\), with \(\mu\) the friction coefficient between the jaw faces and the object, and a safety factor \(S\) covers what the robot adds:

    \[ W = \frac{2\,\mu\,F}{S} \]

    With a conservative \(\mu\) = 0.3 (a hard printed jaw on steel or smooth plastic; a TPU pad or sponge tape on a dry object gives more) and \(S\) = 2, the 60 mm stepper at 76 N holds about 23 N, 2.3 kg, and the 21.5 mm stepper at 31 N about 9 N, 0.9 kg. That is the weight held at rest: a robot accelerating at 1 g doubles the load, and an emergency stop decelerates harder than any programmed move. The friction coefficient is the big unknown; measure it with your jaws on your object, or close on the object's shape (a form-fit grip) and let friction be the reserve.

    Speed¶

    The speed byte of a move is mapped by the firmware to the motor's speed in encoder ticks per second, linearly from 500 at speed 0 to 60000 at speed 255:

    \[ \text{ticks/s} = 500 + \frac{60000 - 500}{255}\, C \]

    The motor's encoder has 16384 ticks per revolution, so the angular speed is \(\omega = \text{ticks/s} / 16384 \times 2\pi\) and each jaw moves at

    \[ v = \omega \, r \]
    Speed byte Motor speed Each jaw moves at The jaws close at
    0 500 ticks/s, 0.03 rev/s 1.6 mm/s 3 mm/s
    30 7500 ticks/s, 0.46 rev/s 24 mm/s 48 mm/s
    128 30400 ticks/s, 1.9 rev/s 97 mm/s 190 mm/s
    255 60000 ticks/s, 3.7 rev/s 190 mm/s 380 mm/s

    Both jaws move, so the gap closes at twice the speed of one jaw. At speed 255 a 105 mm stroke takes about half a second. The gripper calibration runs at speed 40 and 500 mA.

    Position¶

    The position byte is linear over the stroke: 0 is the fully open end stop found by the gripper calibration, 255 the fully closed one. One step of the byte is the stroke divided by 255: 0.22 mm on the 100 mm rail, 0.41 mm on the 150 mm rail, 0.61 mm on the 200 mm rail. The driver considers the position reached within 5 encoder ticks, about 0.03 mm.
